# YouTube plan: Bruce's channel, schedule and first scripts

7 Oct 2026. A companion to `go-to-market.md` (channel 5). Bruce can make videos every week. This plan keeps that
sustainable alongside the repair bench, within the $100 rule (`go-to-market.md` section 5). "Est." marks an estimate.

## 1. One channel: Timing & Tension, with the software in use

**Recommended: one channel, "Timing & Tension: Sewing Machine Repair"** (handle @timingandtension), on Bruce's
repair business. Not a separate software channel.

- Techs and shop owners search for **repair answers**, not software. A repair channel gets found, and the software
  rides along on every video because it really is on the bench.
- A working tech is trusted. A software brand channel is an ad, and nobody subscribes to ads.
- One channel is half the work: one upload schedule, one subscriber count growing.
- Playlists do the sorting: **Repair tips**, **StitchBench at the bench** (demos), **StitchShop for quilt shops**,
  **Switching to StitchShop**, **On the road with Denise**.

Denise's RV-travel channel stays hers. The shops and the travel are her show. When her episode visits a shop that
uses StitchShop, both channels link each other, and Bruce's channel posts a short "on the road" cut. No ad reads
(`go-to-market.md` Q2).

## 6. Search, titles, descriptions, chapters

**Words owners and techs type (from experience; check them in YouTube's search suggestions and in YouTube Studio's
Research tab):** "[brand] sewing machine skipping stitches", "thread bunching under fabric", "bobbin tension", "how
to set sewing machine tension", "sewing machine timing", "sewing machine repair", "vacuum repair", "quilt shop
POS", "point of sale for fabric store", "sewing machine repair software", "repair shop software", "Square for
quilt shop", "Like Sew".

- **Title:** the problem first, the brand and model, under 60 characters. "Brother skipping stitches? Check these 3
  things" beats "StitchBench Episode 2".
- **Description, first two lines:** what the video solves, then "Free 30-day trial, no card: stitchbench.app". The
  same template every time:
  ```
  [One sentence: what this fixes or shows.]
  Try StitchBench / StitchShop free for 30 days, no card: https://stitchbench.app
  Founding shops: $15 / $30 a month for life, first 25 shops.

  Chapters
  0:00 The problem
  0:40 ...
  [last] Logging it in StitchBench

  I'm Bruce, a sewing machine tech in Rhode Island (Timing & Tension, timingandtension.com).
  ```
- **Chapters:** timestamps in the description (the first at 0:00, at least three, each 10 seconds or more). They
  show in search.
- **Tags:** a few only; titles and descriptions do the work.
- **Captions:** always. Older viewers and muted phones read them.

**Thumbnails:** Bruce's face or hands plus the machine, three or four words in big type ("SKIPS STITCHES?"), the
same two colours every week, readable at phone size. No screenshots of the program on long-video thumbnails.

## 11. The first four scripts (short outlines)

### Video 1: "I'm a sewing machine tech, so I built my own shop program" (5-7 min; the channel trailer)
1. **Cold open (10 s):** a hand drops a claim tag on the bench. "Every program I tried was made for phone shops.
   So I built one for sewing machines."
2. **Who I am (30 s):** Bruce, Timing & Tension, Rhode Island; years at the bench; Sewing Doc Academy graduate.
3. **The problem (45 s):** paper tickets, missed pickup calls, hunting for tension specs, quilt shops phoning about
   their customers' machines.
4. **The tour (3 min):** check in a machine (customer, model, problem, tag printed); the tension spec for that
   model; work done on the ticket; the "ready" text goes out; pickup and payment.
5. **The hub (45 s):** the quilt shop sends a repair, it lands on my bench; parts and manuals shared between shops;
   joining is free.
6. **Close (20 s):** "Free 30 days, no card, link below. Next week: skipped stitches on a Brother."

### Video 2: "Brother skipping stitches? Check these 3 things first" (8-10 min)
1. **Hook (10 s):** a stitch line with gaps. "Nine times out of ten it's not the timing."
2. **Check 1, the needle (2 min):** a bent or wrong needle, the right size for the thread, put in all the way.
3. **Check 2, threading (2 min):** presser foot up while threading; the take-up lever.
4. **Check 3, lint and the hook (2 min):** under the needle plate; brush, no canned air.
5. **When it is timing (1 min):** the signs, and when to bring it to a tech.
6. **At the bench (1 min):** the work done goes on the ticket, and the customer gets "your machine is ready" by text.
7. **Close (20 s):** link, subscribe, next week's video.

### Video 3: "Check in a sewing machine in under a minute (StitchBench demo)" (under 2 min, plus 2 Shorts)
1. **Hook (5 s):** a stopwatch on screen. "A customer's waiting. Go."
2. **Look up the customer (10 s):** by phone number; her past machines show up.
3. **The machine (15 s):** model and serial, and its last repair from a year ago.
4. **The problem and estimate (15 s):** her words in her words; the okayed amount.
5. **Print the tag (10 s):** the claim tag on the machine.
6. **Clock stops (5 s):** "48 seconds. 30 days free, link below."

### Video 4: "Thread bunching under the fabric: the real cause" (8-10 min)
1. **Hook (10 s):** a bird's nest under the fabric. "It's almost never the bobbin."
2. **The cause (2 min):** top thread not in the tension discs; why the presser foot matters.
3. **Fix and test (2 min):** rethread with the foot up; a test seam.
4. **When it really is tension (2 min):** a gauge reading in grams; what the factory spec says.
5. **At the bench (1 min):** the program shows the spec for this model next to the reading on the ticket.
6. **Close (20 s):** link and next week (a day at the bench).
