# Go-to-market: getting the word out about StitchBench, StitchShop and StitchHub

7 Oct 2026, for Bruce and Denise. Two people, a launch after the test month. "Est." marks an estimate; everything
else has a source at the end or a file in this repo. Prices and shop counts come from
`docs/plans/pricing-review-2026-10.md` (decided): Solo $25, Shop $45, each extra store $25, founding $15 / $30 for the
first 25 shops or 6 months after paid launch, add-ons (Scheduling $15, Marketing $10, Vac Shop $10, Small Appliance
$5, Maker Shop $10 when built, all add-ons $30), the Assistant $15, a partnered retail shop free, a 30-day trial
without a card, and the written promises (export everything any time; a 12-month price hold with 60 days' notice;
the founding price for the life of the subscription).

**Budget rule (owner, 7 Oct 2026):** $100 for advertising until customer revenue comes in, and no loan. After that,
revenue is rolled back in. So the main plan runs on **time and relationships, not money**. Spending beyond $100 is
a ladder that climbs only as monthly revenue arrives (section 5).

**The short version.** Sell the way this trade already buys: face to face, from someone it trusts, with a friend's
word behind it. Bruce is a working tech and Denise is already in the quilt shops. The partnership loop is the
engine: a repair shop pays and brings its quilt shops free, and a quilt shop brings its tech. Visits on the hop and
RV route, the Sewing Doc Academy community, and a referral month do most of the work. YouTube, Facebook groups, email,
the website and trade-press stories keep the word spreading, all done by the two of them. Paid ads and trade-show
booths come later, paid for by revenue.

## 1. Positioning

### The story

**Easy like Apple, made by a sewing tech.** Bruce fixes machines for a living (Timing & Tension, Rhode Island and
southern New England). He built StitchBench for his own bench, and StitchShop for the quilt shops he works with.
Every screen is held to one rule: someone who is not "a computer person" can use it with a customer at the counter
(`docs/plans/ease-of-use.md`). The trade's own data is built in: tension specs from Bruce's manual library, machine
models and parts.

The market is ready for it. About 3,200 quilt shops remain (down from 5,500 in 2010). Owners are mostly older, and
the typical customer is a retired woman in her 60s. Joann closed in 2025 and sent new customers to independents.
International Quilt Market ended in 2026 after 46 years, so buying and news now move through reps, distributors,
online and word of mouth. The one specialist rival, Like Sew (Rain), starts at $99 a month billed yearly. Owners
complain that it is hard to use, that support is slow and that features "never worked properly".

### Why join the hub (and why it lasts when AI makes software cheap)

Within a few years anyone will be able to have AI write a decent register program. What nobody can copy is **who is
connected to whom**. StitchHub is free to everyone and links:

- **partner repairs:** a quilt shop sends a machine to its tech, both see the ticket, and pickup runs are shared;
- **a parts marketplace** among shops that know each other;
- **the manual library and tension specs**, built by techs for techs.

Every shop that joins makes the hub more useful to every other shop. That is the reason to join now, and the
reason a cheap AI-made program won't replace it. Say it simply: *"The program is the easy part. The people on the
hub are the point."*

### One line per audience

| Audience | The line | Proof to show | Price to say |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repair tech (bench or shop) | "The shop program a sewing tech built for his own bench: check a machine in within a minute, text when it's ready, look up the tension spec, and get your quilt shops' repairs straight onto your bench." | Check-in, ready text, tension lookup, partner job arriving | Solo $25/month; founding $15; 30 days free, no card |
| Quilt / fabric shop owner | "Cut by the yard, sell classes, send repairs to your tech: easy enough for any of your staff on a busy Saturday. Free when your repair shop sets it up." | Register with yardage and fat quarters, class sign-up, "send to my tech" | Free with a real repair partner; otherwise Shop $45, founding $30 |
| Sewing machine dealer | "Machines by serial number, warranty, trade-ins, service plans and your repair bench in one program, every login, $45 a month." | Serial machine sale, warranty at intake, the bench | Shop $45 (+$25 each extra store); founding $30; with all add-ons $75 against $150-250 for Rain Plus or Lightspeed plus a repair tool |
| Vacuum shop | "Repair tickets, the bag/belt/filter finder and central-vac installs, made by someone who works on the bench." | Vac Shop add-on screens | Base plan plus Vac Shop $10 |

Say only what's built. Special orders, customer price groups, loaners, a Notions catalog import and a shop-hop
passport are **not** built (`docs/research/shop-retail-features-and-ai.md`). Never promise them in a pitch.
"It's on the list, and founding shops help decide the order" is the honest answer.

### How to work each channel

**1. The repair-partner loop.** This is the price rule doing the selling. A quilt shop gets StitchShop's Shop plan
free while its repair partner pays and real repairs pass between them (HANDOFF item 22). So:
- Every tech who signs up is asked: "Which quilt shops send you machines? We'll set them up free." Bruce does the
  setup call with the tech on the line.
- Every quilt shop Denise meets is asked: "Who fixes your customers' machines?" If the tech isn't on the hub yet,
  the shop invites them. Bruce follows up as one tech to another.
- Start at home: the quilt shops Timing & Tension already serves are the first partnered shops and the first
  stories.

**2. Denise on the hops and the RV route.** This already works (the All New England Quilt Shop Hop, Oct 2026: 58
shops across five states). The routine is in memory (`shop-hop-scouting`): a passport photo goes in, and a
researched card for each shop comes out in her tour. Add:
- A visit card for each shop: owner name, what they run now (Square, Like Sew, paper), what hurt, next step, and
  the date for a follow-up email.
- The ask is small: "Can Bruce show you 10 minutes on a call this week?" Don't sell on a hop day. Owners are busy;
  buy something, leave a flyer, and book the call.
- Shop Hop Inc. runs 16+ state hops over 30+ states. Plan the RV route around them, so a month of travel passes
  20-60 shops that are expecting visitors.

**3. Sewing Doc Academy.** This follows the launch checklist. Attend the free member open houses and listen. Then
write to Andi Barney as a graduate who built a tool for graduates. Offer founding prices to her certified techs and
a short guest session ("running the business side of a bench"). The goal for later is to have StitchBench
recommended in the Academy's business school. Offer, don't push; no commission scheme at first.

**4. Referrals and the founding offer.**
- **Referral reward (recommended):** when a referred shop pays its first month, both shops get one free month. Up to
  12 free months a year for the shop that refers. A founding shop that refers keeps its locked price, and the free
  month comes off it. It costs no cash and only pays out when a shop pays.
- **Founding offer:** 25 places at $15 / $30 a month, locked for the life of the subscription. It closes at 25
  shops or 6 months after paid launch, whichever comes first (pricing review, decided). Show "places left" and the
  closing date on the site (`FOUNDING_SPOTS_LEFT` already exists). The 30-day trial needs no card, so the ask is
  only "try it".
- Ask at the moment of thanks: after a shop's first good week, Bruce asks, "Who else should have this?"

**5. Own YouTube channel.** Use a channel separate from Denise's RV channel (see Q2), with short videos in Bruce's
voice:
- Repair tips first, software second: "Why your Brother skips stitches," with the fix logged on a StitchBench
  ticket at the end. Techs search for repair tips, not for software.
- 2-minute demos: "Check in a machine in 60 seconds," "Text a customer her machine is ready," "Send a repair to your
  tech from the quilt shop."
- One a week, filmed at the bench with a phone. Captions on, big text, slow pace.
- The tie-in to the RV channel: when Denise's video visits a shop, the description links Bruce's channel. Now and
  then an episode shows Bruce fixing a shop's machine on the road.

**6. Sewing repair YouTubers.** Wes (Sewing Machine Repair Guy, about 85K subscribers) has the tech tour, sent 5
Oct. The owner does **not** want a paid plug. Follow up once with a founding account offered free for a year, and
no ask beyond "tell me what's wrong with it". If he likes it, he may mention it, which is worth more than an ad. Do
the same with two or three smaller repair channels.

**7. Facebook groups: the rules.**
- Read each group's rules first. Most ban self-promotion or allow it only on a set day or thread. Breaking that
  gets a ban, and owners remember.
- Be a useful tech: answer repair and tension questions as Bruce, a working tech, with the business in the
  profile, not in the post.
- Promote only where it's allowed: the group's promo day, or groups that welcome vendors. Post a short video or a
  story, never just a link.
- The state shop-hop groups run all year. Denise posts there as a hopper (photos of shops she loved), not as a
  vendor.
- Never post as two people agreeing with each other.

**8. Email.** Every visit, call or booth conversation gets a follow-up within 2 days: one paragraph, one link, and a
named next step. Then a short monthly note to shops met (with their permission): one tip, one new feature, one shop
story. Send it from the hub's mail or a free Mailchimp tier (free up to 250 contacts).

**9. Website and SEO.** stitchbench.app stays `LIVE = false` until the test month ends. Then add:
- **/compare/like-sew**: an honest table. Like Sew has QuickBooks, loyalty, CommentSold live sales and a Notions
  catalog import. StitchShop has repair and partner shops built in, a hub, a lower price and every feature on both
  plans. Link the sources.
- **/quilt-shop-pos**, **/sewing-machine-repair-software**, **/vacuum-repair-software**: one page each, in shop
  words, each with a 2-minute video.
- **/switch**: the switching guide (section 3).
- A free Google Business Profile for StitchBench, with reviews from the test shops.
Expect little search traffic in 90 days. The site's real job is to make a visit or a referral look trustworthy.

**10. Trade press.** Pitch stories, not ads. FabShop News reaches about 10,000 independent fabric, quilt, machine and
yarn retailers six times a year (a quarter page is $650 and a full page $1,900). Its "Tech Talk" column and "shops
that overcame" stories fit a tech building his own register. The Craft Industry Alliance has 2,000+ members, a
members' Facebook group and a podcast, and a craft-business founder story suits it. Pitch after the test shops can
be named.

**11. Distributors and reps.** Notions Marketing (the largest creative-arts distributor) already has a catalog
integration with Rain. That is a moat for Like Sew until StitchShop can load distributor catalogs. Wait until 10
retail shops are paying and the distributor list import is built. Then ask Notions, and Moda / United Notions, for
catalog data access, and ask reps if they'd mention StitchShop to shops that ask. Don't ask for co-marketing yet.

**12. Paid ads.** None from the first $100 (print does more, section 5). At $1,000 a month of revenue, test
Facebook at $100-150 a month: a 30-second test-shop story video, aimed at quilt shop and sewing machine repair
interests in the RV route's states. Stop if it costs more than $150 per trial. Search ads for B2B software run $5-14
a click and the niche terms have little volume, so skip Google Ads until the SEO pages show which words people use.

## 5. Budget: $100 now, then reinvest as revenue arrives

### The main track: $0 to $100

Everything in sections 2 to 4 except paid ads and booths runs on time: visits and hops, the partnership loop,
referrals, the founding offer, YouTube and Facebook done by Bruce and Denise, Sewing Doc Academy, and email. Free
tools cover the rest: a phone for video, Canva and Clipchamp, Mailchimp's free tier (up to 250 contacts), a Google
Voice number, a Google Business Profile, and the hosted demo company on a tablet they already own. Travel is
already happening, so it isn't counted.

**What the $100 buys first, in this order (est. prices):**

| # | Buy | About | Why first |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 250 two-sided flyers (counter and hop-bag size) with a QR code to the site | $45 | Every visit leaves something on the counter. Older owners keep paper |
| 2 | 250 business cards for Bruce and Denise ("Timing & Tension · StitchBench / StitchShop", QR code) | $20 | For every handshake and hop |
| 3 | 3D printer filament for about 100 bobbin clips or spool holders with the QR code | $20 | Something an owner uses at the cutting table keeps the name in sight; Bruce prints them |
| 4 | Keep $15 | $15 | Reprints, or postage for a few flyer packs mailed to a tech's quilt shops |

No ads from the first $100: a flyer handed over in person converts far better for this audience than a click.

### The ladder: spend from revenue, one step at a time

Monthly revenue (MRR) means customer subscriptions after Stripe fees. Each step is paid from revenue already coming
in, never ahead of it. A step that fails its check (in brackets) is stopped and the money goes to the next one.

| When MRR reaches (est. paying shops) | Add each month or once | About | Check |
|---|---|---|---|
| $250 (about 10) | Once: a small banner and tablecloth for visits and hop tables; a lavalier mic and phone tripod | $180 once | Better videos and tables |
| $500 (about 20) | 500 more flyers; Mailchimp paid tier only if the list passes 250 | $50-75 a month | Visits still win trials |
| $1,000 (about 35-40) | Facebook ads test: test-shop story videos in the route's states | $100-150 a month | Under $150 per trial, or stop |
| $2,000 (about 70) | Trade press: a FabShop News quarter page ($650) or a sponsor slot at FabShop's Virtual Schoolhouse, only if a free story pitch didn't land; walk VDTA or AQS Hartford as visitors | $650 once plus travel | Trials named "saw it in FabShop" |
| $3,000 (about 100) | A trade-show booth: AQS QuiltWeek Hartford (about $1,200 booth, est.) or VDTA/SDTA (dealers) | $1,500-2,500 once with travel and the kit | 10+ trials from the show |
| $5,000 (about 150, the first goal) | Part-time help with setups and support calls, so Bruce's hours go to the bench and the road | Owner's call | Hours per shop falling |

A simple rule of thumb: put back about 15-20% of revenue until the $5,000 goal, then decide again.

## 6. What to measure, and the targets

### The measures (one shared spreadsheet, updated weekly)

| Measure | How | Target (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Conversations (visits, calls, booth chats) | Denise's visit cards, Bruce's call log | 15-25 a week on road weeks |
| Demos | The calendar | 4-8 a week |
| Demo to trial | Trials ÷ demos | 40% or better |
| Trial sign-ups | The hub's admin page | 2-5 a week |
| Trial to paid (30-day trial, no card) | The hub's billing standing | 35-50% (est.; no-card trials convert lower; the test month sets the real base) |
| Partnered shops per paying tech | The hub | 1 or more |
| Referral rate | Paying shops that referred one or more ÷ paying shops | 20% in the first year |
| Cash cost per paying shop | Spend ÷ new paying shops | Under $10 on the main track; under $75 on the ladder |
| Monthly loss of shops | Cancellations ÷ paying shops | Under 2% (the pricing review expects 1.5-3%) |
| Hours per paying shop | Hours logged ÷ new shops | Falling month by month |

### The path to the income goals

The pricing review puts the goals at **about 150 paying shops for $5,000 a month** and **about 230 for $8,000**,
after $383 a month of fixed costs (60/40 Solo/Shop, 30% yearly, $10 of add-ons a shop, 25 founding shops;
partnered free shops roughly pay for themselves through add-ons). It calls 4-8 new paying shops a month after the
founding 25 realistic, which reaches $5,000 around month 18-30 and $8,000 around month 30-45. This plan aims higher
by making the partner loop and referrals carry half the work.

| Milestone | Realistic (pricing review) | Stretch (this plan works) | New paying shops a month (stretch) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 days after launch | 25-30, plus partnered shops | 35, plus 10+ partnered | about 12 |
| 6 months | 45-55 | 70 | about 12 |
| 12 months | 70-100 | 150 ($5,000 goal) | about 13 |
| $8,000 goal (about 230) | month 30-45 | month 18-20 | about 13 |

**What 13 new paying shops a month takes (est.):** at 40% demo to trial and 45% trial to paid, about 72 demos and 29
trials a month, or 3-4 demos every working day. Two people can do that only if referrals and the partner loop bring
half the demos. That is why channels 1 and 4 matter most. On the realistic pace (6 a month), it is about one demo a
day.

**Go-to-launch gate (end of test month):** both test shops used it every working day for the last two weeks, there
is a quote and one number from each, and no open bug that loses money or data.

## 7. Owner questions (recommended answers first)

1. **When is the public launch?** *Recommended: early January 2027.* Quilt and machine shops are at their busiest
   in November and December and won't change registers then. January is inventory and planning time. Use December
   for a quiet start with repair techs (Sewing Doc Academy, Wes) and partnered shops. The founding offer's 6 months
   then run to early July.
2. **Does Denise's RV channel show the software?** *Recommended: lightly.* The shops and the travel are the show.
   The program appears only when it is the story (setting up a shop she visited, Bruce fixing a machine on the
   road). No ad reads. Demos live on a separate StitchBench channel, linked in the descriptions.
3. **A trade show in year one?** *Recommended: no booth;* walk AQS QuiltWeek Hartford (4-7 Aug 2027) on a day ticket,
   and VDTA/SDTA only if the route passes Las Vegas in February. Book a booth when revenue reaches $3,000 a month.
4. **Referral reward: one free month for each side?** *Recommended: yes,* up to 12 free months a year per shop,
   given when the new shop's first payment clears. It costs no cash.
5. **A phone number for support and sales?** *Recommended: yes.* Use a free Google Voice number answered at set
   hours, with texts welcome. Older owners expect to call a person, and slow support is the main complaint about the
   rival.
6. **Approach Notions Marketing and Moda now?** *Recommended: after 10 paying retail shops* and the distributor list
   import, asking for catalog data, not a marketing deal.
7. **Is the $100 spent on print and giveaways rather than ads?** *Recommended: yes* (section 5). Ads start at $1,000
   a month of revenue.
