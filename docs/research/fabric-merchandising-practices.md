# How independent quilt shops manage fabric: research notes

Date: 3 Oct 2026. Public web sources only; nobody contacted. "Not found" means a search and one or two page fetches turned up nothing solid. Source quality is flagged: shop-owner or trade sources are stronger than business-plan template sites, which are marked weak. Several claims come from search-result summaries rather than a full page read; those are marked "search summary".

## 1. Fat quarters

- Shops do both. Fat quarters are the most common precut in quilt shops, and independent shop owners cut many themselves, while manufacturers also sell pre-cut fat quarters and bundles. Some shops cut them from the bolt, some only when not busy, some never; some set a minimum cut of 1/2 yard. https://www.nationalquilterscircle.com/post/what-is-a-precut and https://sewing.patternreview.com/SewingDiscussions/topic/100147 (the PatternReview page returned 403 to me; search summary only).
- Yield: 4 fat quarters per yard (quilters' math: the price of 4 FQ equals a yard). https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/fat-quarters-yardage-t129213.html . Size is 18" x 22" per https://quiltingroomwithmel.com/precut-quilting-fabrics-sizes-names-and-average-cost/ . A 44-45" bolt width halves to 22".
- Price: FQs carry one flat price per fabric line, not the bolt's per-yard price, and cost more than yardage unless on sale. https://www.quiltingboard.com/main-f1/fat-quarters-yardage-t129213.html . One survey of online prices: average yardage $11.71/yd versus fat quarters $16.04/yd equivalent, about 37% more. https://quiltingroomwithmel.com/precut-quilting-fabrics-sizes-names-and-average-cost/ . Individual FQs commonly $3 to $6.50 (https://lindas.com/blogs/quilting-education/fat-quarters-explained-cost-sizes-and-where-to-buy-quality-bundles-online, search summary).
- Margin: fabric gross margin 40-60% (weak, template site: https://financialmodel.net/blogs/owner-makes/quilt-shop-fabrics). "100% markup is standard" (wholesale is half of retail) per a quilting site, not a trade source: https://www.madeurban.com/blog/quilting-price-formula/ . Margin of FQ versus yardage specifically: not found. Whether FQs are "a top money-maker": not found (only the claim that precuts "can command a higher price point").
- Shops keep bins of already-cut fat quarters: when a customer asks for one, the shop cuts the other FQ too and bins it (search summary, original forum not fetched).

## 2. Bolt ends and slow fabric

- The last yard or so is taken off the bolt and sold as a whole piece to make room for new arrivals; used for bindings, borders, backings. https://myfavoritequiltstore.com/collections/end-of-bolt and https://loulousfabricshop.com/collections/bolt-ends (shop pages).
- Scrap bags are made from kit-cutting leftovers and end-of-bolt strips: https://www.littlequiltstore.com/products/scrap-bag . "End of Bolt boxes" with 1-4 yard cuts, 30+ yards: https://www.missouriquiltco.com/products/november-eob-box .
- Clearance depth seen on shop sites: roughly 25% to 50% off; some shops require a 1-yard minimum on clearance and charge full price per yard under a yard. See https://thequiltyshop.com/collections/clearance , https://www.fatquartershop.com/quilting-clearance-sale and https://pearlsandcloversquiltshop.com/collections/clearance (search summary). Fabric lines are often printed once, so older collections get discounted to make room.
- Age thresholds and fixed schedules: not found. American Quilt Retailer says only that "the first markdown you take is always the cheapest markdown," that seasonal sales should start before the season ends, and that cleared stock should move to a marked clearance area at the back. It gives no percentages or ages. https://www.americanquiltretailer.com/taking-the-mystery-out-of-markdowns/ . Like Sew says discounts should be "deep enough to move product but not so deep that you're losing money" with no numbers: https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shop-inventory .
- Like Sew (vendor blog) says some bolts "have been there for years, tying up cash" and urges fewer SKUs: https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shops-fewer-skus . It claims poor inventory management can cost 20-30% of annual revenue (vendor claim, unsourced): https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shop-inventory .

## 3. Kits

- A kit is a pattern plus coordinated fabric cut and bundled; Block of the Month (BOM) programs split a quilt into 9-12 monthly kits. Shops often add finishing kits (sashing, borders, backing, batting) as extras. https://hcquilts.com/blogs/tips-and-tricks/quilt-block-of-the-month and https://www.missouriquiltco.com/blogs/missouri-star-blog/block-of-the-month-program (search summaries).
- BOM pricing tiers (vendor blog): $15-20/month basic, $25-35 midrange, $40-50 premium. Cost should include fabric, cutting and packaging labor, pattern or license fees, and packaging. Reserve all fabric for the whole program before launch, order 5-10% extra, take deposits or autopay. https://likesew.com/blog/block-of-the-month-programs
- Whole-quilt kit prices on shop sites run from about $44 to $500+ (search summary of https://www.fatquartershop.com/quilt-kits and similar). A kit pricing rule of thumb (such as a markup over fabric plus a cutting fee): not found.
- Pattern sources: manufacturers publish free patterns that shops pair with the fabric. Fat Quarter Shop offers "FREE Riley Blake patterns provided by Riley Blake" https://www.fatquartershop.com/free-quilt-patterns/free-quilt-patterns-from-riley-blake-designs-fabric . Independent designers sell patterns; BOMs often use custom patterns (Like Sew BOM link above).
- Licensing: copyright covers the pattern text and illustrations, not the physical product made from it, so a buyer may sell finished items without permission; but a kit that includes the instructions copies or distributes them, so shops use licenses or buy patterns from designers. https://whileshenaps.com/can-you-copyright-a-sewing-pattern/ and https://www.nationalquilterscircle.com/video/quilting-copyright-rules-012110/ (both search summaries). Whether shops "buy one pattern per kit": not found as a stated rule. I found no source saying so, either way. This is not legal advice; StitchShop should let a shop record the pattern source and cost on each kit and not assume rights.

## 4. Reordering

- Collections are short-lived: within about a year of first printing a reorder may be possible; older than that there is "no chance" of finding it wholesale. https://whileshenaps.com/buy-quilting-fabric-wholesale/ (a sewing-business blog, not a trade body).
- A bolt of quilting cotton is 15 yards (44-45" wide), about $82.50-87 wholesale. Opening-order minimums range $0-$5,000, many $500-1,500; Checker: $300 opening, $150 per later order, $1,000 annual; Birch: $450 with no reorder minimum. Distributors (Checker, EE Schenck, Midwest Textiles) let a shop buy a few bolts from many manufacturers, but not every manufacturer sells through them. Same page. Half-bolt minimums: not found.
- Staple rule from a vendor blog: keep a minimum of three bolts per color of bestsellers (Kona solids) before reordering; review sales reports monthly; count inventory quarterly. https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shop-inventory . Sell-through targets or weeks-of-supply benchmarks for quilt shops: not found.
- Turn is measured as a year of retail sales divided by year-end inventory at retail (American Quilt Retailer, search summary of https://www.americanquiltretailer.com/taking-the-mystery-out-of-markdowns/).
- Lead times: only "order fall fabrics in June for August arrival" (Like Sew, above). Concrete weeks: not found. Preorders: online shops sell upcoming collections with estimated arrival dates, e.g. https://myfavoritequiltstore.com/collections/pre-order . How shops place preorder quantities with vendors: not found.
- Trade association for independent shops: Fabric Shop Network, https://fabshopnet.com/us-distributors/ .

## 5. What software offers

Rain Retail (https://www.rainpos.com/quilt-and-fabric-store-pos/)
- Marketed: fractional yards and precuts, cutting table and checkout, serialized products, class management, SMS, Notions Marketing integration, "data-driven reports".
- Not found on that page: bolts, fat-quarter conversion, kit building, markdown suggestions, reorder points, purchase orders.

Like Sew (https://likesew.com/ ; plans from $99/month)
- Marketed: tracks by bolt, cut, color and collection; fractional yardage; reorder alerts; fat quarters, charm packs, jelly rolls, layer cakes; kit bundles with component tracking and automatic stock deduction; "pricing flexibility and markdown options"; custom reports; e-commerce sync; class management; supplier integrations. Automatic purchase orders when a bolt runs low (blog).
- Not found: automatic markdown suggestions; a named sell-through or weeks-of-supply report; converting one cut yard into 4 FQs (precuts are listed, the conversion is not described).

Square for Retail
- Decimal quantities and yard units via the item unit type; one forum answer set stock in 1/4 yards with 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 sizes from the same inventory. https://community.squareup.com/t5/Online-Store/selling-fabric-by-the-yard/m-p/785828

## 6. Sales mix and margins

- Quilting in America / Craft Industry Alliance 2024 (37,000 respondents): 61% prefer local quilt shops; 48% buy fabric locally and 25% online; shop choice driven by fabric assortment, location within 30 minutes, and classes; inflation pushes quilters toward using stash. https://craftindustryalliance.org/the-size-of-the-quilting-market-quilting-trends-survey-results-2024/ . Shop revenue mix is not in that page.
- Mix and margin figures come only from business-plan template sites (weak, likely modeled, not shop data): fabric 60-70% of revenue at 50-60% gross margin, notions 10-20% of sales at 60-80% margin, classes 20-30% of revenue at 80-90% margin, patterns 50-70% margin, net profit 10-20%. https://startupfinancialprojection.com/blogs/profitability/quilt-shop and https://dojobusiness.com/blogs/news/fabric-store-profit-margin (search summaries). Machine sales share: not found. No American Quilt Retailer or Fabric Shop Network figure was found.

## What this means for StitchShop

Ranked, most important first:

1. Fractional yardage everywhere (sale, receiving, counts, purchase orders): sell to 1/8 yard and receive to hundredths, because bolts arrive at 15.68 yards, not 15. This is where Square struggles.
2. Bolt-level tracking: a bolt as a unit with yards remaining, cut-and-subtract at the register, and an "end of bolt" flag at a shop-set threshold that offers one-click actions (sell as remnant, cut into fat quarters, move to scrap or kit stock).
3. Fat-quarter conversion: cut one yard (or half) into 1-4 FQs with a flat FQ price per line, separate from the per-yard price, and a count of pre-cut FQs kept linked to the bolt. Four per yard.
4. Collection-aware inventory: group bolts by collection and show weeks since arrival, yards on hand and sell-through per collection and colorway, since lines are printed once and the reorder window is about a year.
5. A markdown assistant that suggests, never auto-applies: shop-set age and sell-through rules, clearance tiers, a clearance location, and a 1-yard minimum option. No benchmark was found, so rules stay configurable with no hard-coded defaults (shops advertise 25-50%).
6. Kits and BOM: a kit as pattern plus cut components that deducts fabric on sale, with a pattern source and license cost line, a cutting-labor fee, and BOM subscriptions (monthly kit, deposit, autopay, 5-10% extra fabric reserved for the whole program).
7. Reorder and preorder support: vendor minimums (opening, per-order, annual), a staple minimum-bolts rule per color, reorder suggestions that skip collections past their reorder window, and preorder lines for upcoming collections with expected dates.
8. Reports owners are told to watch: inventory turn at retail, monthly sell-through by collection, and cash tied up in dead stock. Sales mix by department (fabric, notions, patterns, classes, machines) is easy to offer; no public benchmark exists.
