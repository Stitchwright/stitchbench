Open an account online, or call 1-800-537-1060 or email custservice@checkerdist.com ([same page](https://www.checkerdist.com/info_business.cfm?direct=1)). Over 125,000 products, real-time stock checks ([Checker](https://www.checkerdist.com/info_chooseus.cfm?direct=1), search summary). Catalog is integrated into MicroBiz ([MicroBiz](https://www.microbiz.com/sewing-fabric-store-pos-system/)) and Like Sew ([Like Sew](https://likesew.com/integrations)). - **Brewer Quilting and Sewing Supplies**: wholesale account application on its site; 1-800-676-6543, info@brewersewing.com; says it has served independent shops since 1914 (search summary of [Brewer](https://www.brewersewing.com/)). - **Notions Marketing** (Grand Rapids): the strongest integration story. ## Using manufacturer images (summary)

- Images are mostly available to account holders: Moda (JPG zips), Kaufman (Dropbox, customers only), Andover (zips, no login shown), Benartex (login), Riley Blake (by request), Windham (marketing assets), Art Gallery (by email).
- Stated terms are thin. Best statements: Moda says the JPGs are for blogs and websites; Riley Blake welcomes customers sharing its images and asks for tags; Andover asks for tags; Art Gallery requires approval for uses beyond display and bans reproducing the designs. Nobody I found says anything explicit about use inside a POS or stock screens, and nobody states a rule on resale or on copying images to the shop's own server.
- Riley Blake adds a MAP price policy that matters for any shop price display, and bars finished goods from licensed lines.
- Practical rule for StitchShop: treat images as the shop's own copy under that shop's account, ask each manufacturer in writing to confirm that display in a shop's POS, website and stock screens is allowed, and keep the source and tags where a maker asks for them.

## 4. Patterns and project sheets

- **Moda Bake Shop** hosts free patterns using precuts ([Moda Bake Shop](https://my.modafabrics.com/categories/moda-bake-shop)). Search summaries say items made from the patterns are for personal, non-commercial use unless the design owner gives written permission (search summary; the actual terms page was not found). Contact listed in a search summary: modabakeshop@modafabrics.com. The Moda downloads page also lists free patterns per collection ([downloads](https://my.modafabrics.com/downloads)).
- **Riley Blake**: over a hundred free quilt patterns and free project panel patterns ([panel patterns](https://www.rileyblakedesigns.com/free-project-panel-patterns)); PDFs carry "All Rights Reserved" ([sample PDF](https://www.rileyblakedesigns.com/assets/images/freepatterns/quiltpatterns/LittleThingsFreePattern.pdf)). Retailers sell kits of its free patterns (Fat Quarter Shop lists them, [link](https://www.fatquartershop.com/free-quilt-patterns/free-quilt-patterns-from-riley-blake-designs-fabric)). Written kit terms: not found.
- **Andover/Makower** offers free patterns and notes many collections have a free download ([wholesale info](https://andoverfabrics.com/wholesale-info/)).
- **Pattern distributors**: Checker, United Notions and EE Schenck carry independent publishers' patterns; wholesalers typically require a barcode and fabric requirements on the pattern ([Quilty Love wholesale](https://www.quiltylove.com/wholesale/), search summary). **Quilt in a Day** lists wholesale customers; 1-800-777-4852 ([site](https://quiltinaday.com/), search summary).
- **Kit licensing in general**: copyright exists without a notice, and selling kits or copies of a pattern needs the publisher's written permission; a finished item from a purchased pattern is a separate question ([SAQA](https://www.saqa.com/members/member-benefits/copyrights-and-quilting) and [Kathleen Bissett](http://www.kathleenbissett.com/How%20Copyright%20Affects%20the%20Quilter.pdf), both search summaries). Each publisher's own terms rule. Not legal advice.
- **Pattern data feed or API with fabric requirements per pattern**: not found at any publisher. Fabric requirements exist only inside the PDFs.

## 5. Who to approach first

(a) Catalog import with images:
1. **Moda / United Notions**: biggest quilt-shop supplier, a public JPG-per-collection library with swatch PDFs and in-store dates, and an existing Like Sew integration proving a catalog hand-off exists.
2. **Checker Distributors**: 125,000+ products across many brands in one catalog, already integrated with Like Sew and MicroBiz, so a feed exists in some form.
3. **Notions Marketing**: modern B2B platform with a product-information system, CSV and an existing Rain integration; also the one with real purchasing and receiving automation.
4. **Robert Kaufman**: seasonal image folders already organized, Kona solids are a staple; no integration yet found, so a data hand-off would be new.
5. **Riley Blake**: friendliest stated image policy, a repository on request, clear release dates; needs a request, and MAP rules apply.

(b) Electronic ordering:
1. **Notions Marketing** (CSV bulk import, ERP connection, existing POS integration, EDI mentioned).
2. **Checker Distributors** (stock checks, backorder status, integrated by two POS makers).
3. **Moda / United Notions** (one wholesale site, one account, many lines; ordering part of its Like Sew integration is not stated).
4. **Brewer** (integrated with two POS makers; ordering side not stated).
5. **Jaftex group (FreeSpirit, Henry Glass, Studio E, Blank)**: one account across four companies, so one conversation covers four lines; no integration found.

Order of approach overall: Notions Marketing and Checker for ordering plus catalog, Moda for fabric images, then Riley Blake and Kaufman.
