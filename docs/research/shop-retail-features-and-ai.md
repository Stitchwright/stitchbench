# Close the special-order gap before anything else

StitchShop already covers more of what a quilt shop, fabric store or sew-and-vac dealer needs than any rival we found, so the remaining gaps are narrow and specific. The most important one is **special orders and pre-orders**: a "coming soon" item with a small deposit, rolled into the shop's own purchase order, with a text to the customer when it arrives. Every serious competitor has this (Rain, MicroBiz, Lightspeed), shops already take $10-$25 pre-order deposits by hand, and quilters rank "is it in stock" above price. Three more gaps belong in the launch package. The first is **automatic prices for a group of customers** (guild, teacher, club card, birthday, a lower price per yard on big cuts). The second is **loaner and demo machines tracked by serial number**. The third, from the AI list, is **reading a photographed packing slip or invoice into receiving**, which Rain's quilt edition and Lightspeed both shipped in 2025-26. After those come channel work that lifts sales (Google "in stock nearby" listings, Facebook and Instagram catalogs pointing at the shop's own web store, a simple live-sale claim list), event tools (shop hops, retreats), and dealer paperwork (warranty claims, co-op ads, MAP-safe web prices). On AI, three ideas are now table stakes that customers will expect: invoice reading, "ask your shop a question" and marketing drafts. The real differentiator is **"build this quilt from what's on our shelves"**, which no product we found does. Any assistant that talks to customers directly must answer only from the shop's own records, because a 2024 ruling made a business liable for what its chatbot said.

How this list was made: five research notes from 7 Oct 2026 (competitors, owner complaints, industry practice, sales channels and dealer repair, AI), checked against StitchShop's current code and the built, being-built and planned lists. Several notes' "missing" items turned out to be **already built**: review-request texts, longarm rental by the hour with a certification check, class waitlists, vacuum trade-ins, web orders for pickup or shipping with tracking, and the books export. They are marked that way below and are not counted as gaps.

## Four gaps every launch customer will notice

The tables use three effort hints. Small is days of work, medium is one to three weeks, and large is a month or more or something that needs an outside partner's approval.

| # | Gap | Who needs it | Effort | Status |
|---|-----|--------------|--------|--------|
| 1 | Special orders, pre-orders ("coming soon" with deposit) and want lists with arrival alerts | All four shop types | Medium | Gap |
| 2 | Customer price groups: guild, teacher, club card, wholesale; birthday offers; per-yard price breaks; simple loyalty | Quilt and fabric shops mostly; dealers for lifetime vacuum discounts | Medium | Gap (scheduled promotions are being built, but those are by date, not by customer) |
| 3 | Loaner, demo, rental and trial machines by serial number, checked out against a repair ticket or customer | Dealers and repair shops | Small to medium | Gap. Trade-ins are **already built** for vacuums; check that the machines-for-sale work **being built** carries trade-ins over to sewing machines |
| 4 | Photo of a packing slip or invoice fills in receiving (see the AI section) | All shops that buy stock | Medium | Gap |

**Special orders and pre-orders come first** because they touch every kind of shop, every competitor has them, and owners lose sales without them. Rain includes "special order tracking" and "quote management" in its cheapest $99-a-month tier ([Rain pricing](https://www.rainpos.com/pricing)). MicroBiz tracks special orders and back orders from the order through receiving to the sale ([MicroBiz](https://www.microbiz.com/?p=43118)), and Lightspeed has them free in its register ([Lightspeed vs Square](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/au/pos/lightspeed-vs-square/)). A quilt shop reviewing Rain asked specifically for a **pre-order or "coming soon" product status** ([Capterra Rain reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/140893/Rain-Point-of-Sale/reviews/?page=2)). Shops already work this way by hand: **$10 non-refundable deposits** to pre-register for a kit subscription, and **$25 deposits with the balance billed about a week before the kit ships** ([My Girlfriend's Quilt Shoppe](https://mygirlfriendsquiltshoppe.com/products/2026-quiltlet-subscription-pre-registration-deposit), [Bella Rose Quilts](https://bellarosequilts.com/products/pre-order-wrap-it-up-quilt-kit-featuring-berry-pine-from-vanessa-goertzen-of-lella-boutique)). Customers care too. In the 2025 Quilter's Survey (over 30,000 responses), **assortment and availability ranked first** among reasons to choose a shop, ahead of price, and **64% of supply purchases** were made in person ([FabShop News, June 2025](https://fabshopnet.com/downloads/Todays-Quilting-Trends-June2025.pdf)). StitchShop already has most of the parts. Purchase orders, parts-arrived texts on repair tickets and layaway deposits (being built) exist, so the work is joining them up: one record holds the customer, the item, the deposit and the expected date; it rolls into the next purchase order; and receiving sends the "it's here" text. A want list (tell me when the next Tula Pink line lands) is the same record without a deposit. A wish list or gift registry, which Rain sells in its top tier, can wait until want lists are in use.

**Customer price groups** are the second gap. In quilt shops the discount follows the person, not the calendar. MicroBiz sells **guild and wholesale price levels** and **lower per-yard prices at 10 yards and up** ([MicroBiz](https://www.microbiz.com/?p=43118)). Nebraska Quilt Company sells annual club memberships that give **15% off any time the card is shown** ([Nebraska Quilt Co.](https://nebraskaquiltcompany.com/products/2024-embroidery-club-membership)). Birthday offers show up in shop newsletters as late as July-August 2026 ([shop newsletter](https://media.rainpos.com/10478/July_and_August_2026_Newsletter.pdf)). Rain, Like Sew and Lightspeed all advertise loyalty programs ([Like Sew](https://www.likesew.com/), [Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/referrals/explore-lightspeed-retail)). StitchShop's customer record today has no birthday, membership or price group. The useful version is small in concept: a customer belongs to a group, the group has a percentage off (with an expiry date for paid memberships), and the register applies it on its own. The same flag covers the vacuum dealer's "**25% off bags, belts and filters for the life of the vacuum**" offer ([Rocky Mountain Sewing & Vacuum, terms unverified](https://www.rockymountainsewing.com/vacuum-cleaner-repair-savings)). Points-style loyalty is a separate decision. No source showed which style quilt shops prefer, so start with a birthday field and member discounts and add points only if customers ask for them.

**Loaner and demo machines** matter to dealers and repair shops. Rain bundles **loaner tracking, trial purchases, short- and long-term rental (rent-to-own) and trade-ins** in its middle tier ([Rain pricing](https://www.rainpos.com/pricing)). Repair shops advertise loaner machines and vacuums to win the job ([Inspired to Sew](https://inspiredtosew.com/blogs/sewing-essentials-getting-started/how-to-choose-the-right-sewing-machine-repair-shop), [vacuum shop listing](https://local.bakersfield.com/bakersfield-us-ca/oreck-clean-home-center-661-835-0333/2018-10-29-9449806-what-kind-of-vacuum-do-you-have-chances-are-we-service-it-we-also-have-bags-filters-belts-and-accessories-for-most-brands-of-vacuums-we-offer-free-estimates-on-repair-quick-turn-around-time-and-loaner-vacuums-available-dont-throw-it-away)). StitchShop already tracks each vacuum for sale by serial number with a status, a condition and the trade-in that brought it in. Adding "loaner", "demo" and "rental" statuses with a check-out date, a due date and a card or deposit on file builds on that record rather than starting over. A **trade-up guarantee** belongs in the same work. B-Sew Inn credits the repair cost toward a new machine within 30 days ([B-Sew Inn](https://www.bsewinn.com/?p=63516)), and one dealer values a trade-in at what it expects to sell for in 60-90 days ([Meissner Sewing](https://meissnersewing.com/blogs/inspiration/whats-your-trade-in-worth?page=2)). That is a dated credit note attached to the ticket.

## Channels and events that bring customers through the door

These are worth building after the launch package. They grow sales rather than fix daily friction, and several set StitchShop apart from Like Sew.

| # | Gap | Who needs it | Effort | Status |
|---|-----|--------------|--------|--------|
| 5 | Google "in stock nearby" product listings fed from inventory | Shops with barcoded notions, patterns, machines, bags | Medium (large if Google partner approval is needed) | Gap |
| 6 | Facebook and Instagram catalog that sends buyers to the shop's own web store | Quilt and fabric shops | Small | Gap |
| 7 | Live-sale claim list (customer, item, quantity, hold stock, text a pay link) | Quilt and fabric shops that sell on Facebook Live | Medium | Gap |
| 8 | Repricing when a vendor raises prices (tariffs), margin alerts, back-order view | All | Small to medium | Gap. Distributor list import is **planned** and should carry new costs |
| 9 | Retreats and ticketed events: payment schedule, early-bird price, room and meal choices, door-prize drawing | Quilt shops | Medium | Gap. Classes with seats and waitlists are **already built**; layaway installments are **being built** |
| 10 | Shop hop passport with digital stamps on StitchHub and prize drawings | Quilt shops in regional hops | Medium to large | Gap, and a differentiator, but unproven |
| 11 | Help switching from Square, Rain/Like Sew or QuickBooks Desktop POS (customer, item and gift-card import) | New customers | Small to medium | Partly built (spreadsheet import) |
| 12 | Card reader and tap-to-pay at the counter, connected to the sale | All | Large | Gap. Today card payments are recorded as a tender; the online shop already uses Stripe |

**Google and Meta listings are cheap reach.** Google shows a shop's in-store stock on Search, Maps and Shopping when the register feeds it. Square and Lightspeed do this free, and Lightspeed **enrolls North American shops automatically** ([Lightspeed](https://product-info.lightspeedhq.com/lightspeed-retail-x-r-series-google-local-inventory-noam), [Square](https://goo.gle/3gi9q8F)). The entry bar is **one physical store and at least 50 products with real barcodes** ([Google Business Profile help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9934993)). No evidence turned up that Like Sew does this ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/449886-Like-Sew)), so it is a visible point of difference. Fabric bolts usually lack those barcodes, so the listings will mostly show notions, patterns, machines and vacuum bags. Meta made the Facebook and Instagram work easier: between June and August 2025 it **moved all US Shops to checkout on the seller's own website** ([ppc.land](https://ppc.land/meta-phases-out-facebook-and-instagram-shops-checkout-by-august-2025/)). A product list file pointing at StitchShop's existing web store is enough, with no Meta payment setup.

**Live sales are where quilt shops already sell online.** CommentSold runs a page aimed at quilt, yarn and craft shops, built around "Sold 2 yards of blue" comments that become orders ([CommentSold](https://try.commentsold.com/arts-crafts/)). Like Sew partnered with CommentSold for shoppable live video ([CommentSold press release](https://try.commentsold.com/?p=19830)). The research found no count of how many shops sell this way, so the evidence is indirect. A plain "live sale" screen is a medium job: staff type each claim, the stock is held, and a pay link goes out by text. Reading Facebook comments automatically needs Meta's approval and is a large job, so it should wait. TikTok Shop takes **8% per sale since August 2026**, plus creator fees ([beBOLD](https://www.bebolddigital.com/news/tiktok-shop-8-percent-referral-fee)), which makes it a poor fit for independents.

**Tariffs make repricing urgent.** Fabric prices rose roughly **10-20%** ([Butler Eagle](https://www.butlereagle.com/?p=105764)). A Michigan dealer warned customers about increases on fabric and "the majority of the sewing machines", and said vendors were holding back reorders ([Lansing State Journal via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/tariffs-hit-east-lansing-sewing-233308590.html)). What helps is a way to raise one vendor's prices by a set percentage in one step, a warning when an item's margin falls below the shop's floor, and a list of what is on back order so staff can tell customers what is coming. This rides on the purchase orders already built and the distributor list import already planned.

**Events: retreats are proven, shop hops are an open field.** Retreats run on **non-refundable deposits of $150-$400**, with the balance due **60-90 days before** the start and early-bird discounts ([Quiltworx](https://www.quiltworx.com/?p=89029), [BobKat retreat](https://new.quiltworx.com/event/bobkats-spring-retreat-in-shipshewana-indiana-2/)). Trunk shows charge **$10-$15 at the door** with door prizes ([Boothbay Register, via search summary](https://www.boothbayregister.com/node/107355)). Classes and layaway together cover about half of a retreat. The missing pieces are a dated payment schedule, room and meal choices, and a drawing. Shop hops are large and regular: **44 Minnesota shops** joined the 2025 statewide hop ([New Ulm Journal](https://www.nujournal.com/news/local-news/2025/07/23/new-ulm-shops-among-44-in-minn-participating-in-state-fair-for-quilting/)), and the 2026 Northern California hop gave a sewing machine as its grand prize to passports stamped at all 10 shops ([Quilt Rush 2026 flyer](https://media.rainpos.com/2374/flyer_2026.pdf)). Row by Row now runs as **Quilters Trek** ([Stitchin' Post](https://stitchinpost.com/blogs/stitchin-post-blog/row-by-row-is-back)). Every hop found still uses **paper passports**, and no register tracks stamps. StitchHub already links shops, so a phone-scanned stamp kept on the hub fits naturally. It is still unproven. Before building it, ask one hop organizer whether they would use it, and make each shop's participation and each shopper's sign-up opt-in, in line with the sharing rule.

**Switching help and card readers.** In a general survey of 400+ merchants, **34%** switched systems over missing features, **27%** over poor support and **26%** over price ([Digital Transactions](https://www.digitaltransactions.net/most-pos-hardware-and-software-providers-compete-for-just-a-sliver-of-retailers/)). A quilt shop owner who left Square for Rain said features she was "sold" "never worked properly" ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140893/Rain-Point-of-Sale/reviews/?page=2)), and another shop moved because QuickBooks discontinued its desktop register ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/449886-Like-Sew)). Ready-made import templates for those systems' export files are a cheap way to win shops that are already looking to move. A card reader connected to the sale is the largest item on this list. Shops can keep their own terminal and record the tender as they do now, so it only becomes urgent if launch customers ask for it. Phone tap-to-pay through Stripe costs **2.7% + 5 cents**, with no monthly fee ([Beancount blog](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/20/tap-to-pay-iphone-android-no-terminal-merchant-cost-guide)).

Dealer contracts stop shops from **advertising a machine below the brand's minimum advertised price (MAP)**, even though in-store prices go lower ([The Sewing Machine Newsletter](https://sewingmachineman.substack.com/p/wading-through-map-msrp-trade-ins)). A web store that shows a deal price can put a dealership at risk. Baby Lock **reimburses up to 100% of qualifying co-op ads** ([Co>Op Connect](https://coopconnect.com/national-product-promotions-are-available-through-coop-connect-baby-lock/)), which is money dealers lose when claims slip. Brother runs a portal where authorized service centers file warranty claims and get paid at set rates ([Brother ASC](https://www.brother-usa.com/-/media/partner-program/brother-asc.pdf)). Those portals are closed, so StitchShop should log and reconcile claims rather than try to connect to them. Each of these is small and keeps dealers loyal, but none decides a sale. Evidence for house accounts and institutional service contracts is thin and mostly from outside the US ([AE Sewing Machines](https://www.ae-sewingmachines.com/pages/school-sewing-and-textile-equipment)), so it sits here until a repair-shop customer asks. Quilt appraisals cost **$55-$75** per quilt ([AQS](https://www.americanquilter.com/connect/aqs-certified-appraisal-program/)) and fit a printed certificate on a service ticket.

The research also confirmed several things StitchShop already has or is building, which are worth knowing as selling points rather than work. These include decimal yardage, fat-quarter conversion and bolt ends, which Square still cannot do ([Square community](https://community.squareup.com/t5/Archived-Discussions-Read-Only/How-are-others-that-price-by-a-decimal-quantities-working-their/m-p/40274/highlight/true)). They also include service plans priced by machine value, which Rain users asked for and dealers such as Quality Sewing sell at **$199-$1,199 for three years** ([Quality Sewing](https://www.qualitysewing.com/pages/cmp)), as well as financing records for the brands' **0% for 36-60 month** sale events ([SewingMachinesPlus](https://sewingmachinesplus.com/blogs/news/baby-lock-annual-summer-sale-is-here-best-prices-of-the-year-on-select-machines-special-financing)), review-request texts, and longarm rental billed by the hour with certification. Rain users report that **Shopify sync turns off class sign-up** ([Capterra Like Sew](https://www.capterra.com/p/10021014/Like-Sew/)), which StitchShop avoids by running its own web store. No source covered rain checks, so they are left off.

## AI: three features customers will expect, one that sets StitchShop apart

Small businesses have taken up AI quickly. A US Chamber survey found **58% used generative AI in 2025, up from 23% in 2023** ([IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/08/18/us-chamber-report-small-businesses-rapidly-adopting-ai-despite-regulatory-concerns/)). Owners trust it least near money and privacy, and in an Intuit survey bookkeeping drew **the most hesitation** ([Firm of the Future](https://www.firmofthefuture.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-impact-report-2026/)). Shopify gives its assistant away free with every plan ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/magic)), so generic AI is not something a shop will pay extra for. The value comes from AI that works on the shop's own data. StitchShop already has a stronger repair-side assistant than any competitor found (work-done drafts, likely causes, reading ID plates, manual answers with page citations, bolt-label reading).

| Rank | AI feature | Table stakes or differentiator | Effort | Why |
|------|-----------|-------------------------------|--------|-----|
| 1 | Photo of a packing slip or invoice fills in receiving and new costs | **Table stakes** (launch) | Medium | Lightspeed shipped it in 2026 ([Lightspeed](https://www.lightspeedhq.com/news/lightspeed-commerce-launches-ai-powered-automation-to-help-retailers-eliminate-manual-inventory-entry/)); Like Sew's "Commerce" assistant scans vendor invoices ([Like Sew](https://www.likesew.com/)); owners complain receiving is inaccurate ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140893/Rain-Point-of-Sale/reviews/?page=2)). It is a short step from the bolt-label reader StitchShop already has |
| 2 | Ask your shop a question in plain English ("which precuts sold best since spring?", "which machines waited longest for parts?") | **Table stakes** | Medium | In Square's beta, **60% of questions were about running the business**, and "tens of thousands" of sellers used it within two months ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-ai-beta-insights)). The numbers must come from real reports, with AI only wording the answer |
| 3 | Marketing and web-store drafts from real data: new-arrivals email, class blurbs, product descriptions, social posts | **Table stakes** | Small | Like Sew ships Marketing and Site assistants ([Like Sew](https://www.likesew.com/)); owners ask for easier email marketing ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/140893/Rain-Point-of-Sale/reviews/?page=2)) |
| 4 | "Build this quilt from our stock": pattern size to yardage, coordinating bolts actually on the shelf, then thread to match | **Differentiator** | Medium to large | Consumer apps work out yardage or colors ([QuiltFit](https://apps.apple.com/mr/app/id6776541705), [Quilt Duck](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6755566364)), but none checks a shop's live inventory. Yardage must be calculated by the program, never guessed by AI. The thread step needs the **planned** thread fields |
| 5 | Trade-in helper: plate photo (built) plus condition photos give a draft note and a suggested value from the shop's own past used-machine sales | **Differentiator** | Small to medium | Fits how dealers value trade-ins ([Meissner Sewing](https://meissnersewing.com/blogs/inspiration/whats-your-trade-in-worth?page=2)). A suggestion for staff only |
| 6 | Plain-English care tips and "what we did" sheet for the customer at pickup | Differentiator (small) | Small | Builds on the existing work-done drafts |
| 7 | One-sentence explanations on fabric insight ("these 12 Christmas prints haven't moved since January") | Nice to have | Small | Fabric insight is **already built**; this only adds wording |
| 8 | Customer-facing answers: "is my machine ready?", hours, classes with seats left, booking a drop-off, for web chat or a bought AI phone service | Differentiator, **with liability caution** | Medium | See below |
| Later | Web listing photo cleanup; counting thread spools from a rack photo; thread match from a phone photo | Not worth building yet | Varies | Google gives photo tools away free ([Google Product Studio](https://blog.google/products/shopping/google-product-studio-generative-ai-product-photos/)) and fabric color accuracy matters more than a nice background. Spool counting is unproven ([MIT LIFT Lab](https://liftlab.mit.edu/?p=436)). Shops still trust physical swatch cards over phone color ([Keepsake Quilting](https://www.keepsakequilting.com/products/aurifil-swatch-book)), so photo matches should be offered only as "pull these and check" |

Two things should not be built at all. Embroidery digitizing already belongs to Wilcom, Hatch and Brother. "Smart" sales forecasting adds little for fabric, because most prints sell slowly and are never reordered. Research says it is "still debatable" whether advanced methods beat simple ones for that kind of item ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14718)). StitchShop's reorder points and fabric ordering are already the right tool.

**The caution on customer-facing AI is real.** In *Moffatt v. Air Canada* (February 2024), a tribunal held the airline **liable for its website chatbot's wrong answer** and rejected the argument that the bot was a separate entity responsible for its own words ([ABA Business Law Today](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/business_law/resources/business-law-today/2024-february/bc-tribunal-confirms-companies-remain-liable-for-ai-chatbot-created-information/)). A shop's bot that guesses a repair price or promises a warranty term binds the shop. The safe design answers **only from the shop's records**: ticket status checked against the ticket number and phone, hours, seats left in a class, and open drop-off times. It hands everything else to a person and never quotes a price or a diagnosis that staff have not entered. Phone answering is better bought than built. In one small-business test an AI phone line turned **43 of 61 previously missed calls into booked jobs**, but callers usually recognized the bot within ten seconds ([Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-phone-receptionist-small-business-test/)). StitchShop should offer a status-and-booking link those services can read from. No data was found on how quilters, mostly retired women in their 60s ([FabShop News](https://fabshopnet.com/downloads/Todays-Quilting-Trends-June2025.pdf)), react to bots, so the feature should stay off unless the shop turns it on, and switching it off should be easy.

## Conclusion

StitchShop's fabric, club, repair and partner-shop features already match or beat the specialist competitor. What remains are counter-side habits shops run on paper today: the special-order slip, the guild discount someone remembers, the loaner tag and the retyped packing slip. Turning those into records the register handles closes the gaps launch customers will notice first, and most of them extend things already built (purchase orders, parts-arrived texts, layaway, vacuum serial tracking, bolt-label reading) rather than adding new areas.

The real differentiation is in what only a connected shop system can do. Answering "build this quilt from our shelves" needs live inventory. Digital shop-hop stamps need a hub that already links shops. "Is my machine ready?" needs repair tickets. Rain, Square and Shopify each lack one of those three. They are also the riskiest ideas, the shop hop being unproven demand and the customer-facing answers carrying legal exposure, so each should start as a small trial with one or two willing shops and grow only if the shops use it.
