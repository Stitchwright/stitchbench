# Fabric competitors and owner pain points

Researched 3 Oct 2026 from public pages only (no sign-ups, no contact). Method note: vendor sites and vendor blogs are marketing, so they show what a vendor claims, not what works. Owner voices were hard to find in public search (no usable Reddit or Facebook posts surfaced, podcast pages gave titles only), so most owner quotes below come from Capterra and Software Advice reviews, one Square community thread and one podcast summary. "Not found" means I did not see it on a public page, not that it is absent.

## 1. Rain Retail and Like Sew are one company

Like Sew is the quilt, fabric and sewing brand of Rain Retail Software. Evidence: Like Sew's own tiers, pricing page and feature wording match Rain's, reviews call it "Rain Retail (Like Sew)", and a quilt shop sells a card reader "for Like Sew/Rain POS". Sources: https://likesew.com/pricing , https://www.rainpos.com/pricing , https://www.nebraskaquiltcompany.com/products/wisepos-e-credit-card-machine-for-like-sew-rain-pos . Treat them as one competitor with two skins. It is the market leader in cloud quilt-shop POS as far as public sources show; a customer-count figure was not found.

### Features claimed (Rain / Like Sew)
- Fractions and precuts: "Manage fractional yards and precuts", "Streamline the cutting table & checkout" (https://www.rainpos.com/quilt-and-fabric-store-pos/). Fat quarters as their own SKU type, bolt-level yardage: not found on those pages.
- Kits and bundles: kit building with automatic tracking (https://www.rainpos.com/blog/craft-store-point-of-sale-software); "Kitting and bundling" (https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shop-inventory-software-features). Core plan lists "classes, kits, precuts, vendor purchasing" (https://likesew.com/pricing).
- BOM clubs: "Subscriptions (BOM)" only on the top Plus plan (https://www.rainpos.com/pricing).
- Classes: online sign-up and text reminders (https://www.rainpos.com/quilt-and-fabric-store-pos/).
- Website sync: integrated e-commerce site, "Enter information in one place and watch all your other channels update in real-time" (same page). Product images supported.
- Vendor catalogs: Notions Marketing integration for supplier orders and product upload (https://www.rainpos.com/blog/craft-store-point-of-sale-software ; https://www.softwareadvice.com/retail/rainpos-profile/reviews/). Other fabric wholesalers (Moda etc.): not found. Like Sew says it can "Import directly from vendor catalogs" (https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/449886-Like-Sew/).
- Purchase orders: "vendor purchasing" on Core; supplier lead times and "Forecasting and demand planning" claimed in a Like Sew blog (https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shop-inventory-software-features). How a PO is built, or whether it suggests quantities from fabric sales: not found.
- Reports: "data driven reports"; customizable reports. Reviewers complain (below).
- AI: product description and SEO text generator, announced Oct 2024, over 10,000 descriptions made in pilot (https://fabshopnet.com/like-sew-announces-artificial-intelligence-feature/). Shows the vendor sees data entry of new fabric as a pain point. Photo or SKU auto-fill: not found.

### Price and contract
- Startup $99 per month billed annually; Core and Plus prices hidden ("talk with an expert") (https://www.rainpos.com/pricing). Unlimited users, products, phone and email support.
- Like Sew says "No. Like Sew is a monthly subscription with no long-term lock-in" (https://likesew.com/pricing). Yet a reviewer cites "Cancellation notice requirements" and being told on cancelling "things were not completed" (https://www.softwareadvice.com/retail/rainpos-profile/reviews/ ; https://www.capterra.com/p/140893/Rain-Point-of-Sale/reviews/?page=2). One review reports onboarding and setup costs of "$2,000+" (Software Advice; one person's report).
- Hardware and payments: sold separately (card reader sold via a quilt shop, see above). Exact payment rates: not found.

### What reviewers praise
- One-time data entry: "We love the time that it saves us by only having to load a product once" (https://www.capterra.com/p/10021014/Like-Sew/reviews/).
- Web and shop stock together: "Seamless integration of Web and Brick & Mortar inventory management" (https://www.capterra.com/p/140893/Rain-Point-of-Sale/reviews/). "keep inventory updated when I am at a trade show or selling product online" (Capterra page 2).
- Real-person phone support in several reviews. Ratings: Rain 4.1 of 5 (134 reviews), Like Sew 4.4 of 5 (28 reviews).

### What reviewers complain about
- Price: "expensive ($100 more than the system I went back to)"; "high monthly fee" (Capterra Rain page 2). "The cost is a bit high for my budget" (Like Sew reviews).
- Website: "The website builder is trash, overpriced for what you get" (1 star, Jan 2025, Software Advice); "primitive" with "limited templates" (Like Sew reviews).
- Reports: "has a difficult time with reporting large samples of data like a year's sales" (Like Sew, Capterra); "The Sales Tax report is the worst, it does not break out non taxable items" (Software Advice).
- Inventory: "Inventory management at year-end needs improvement" (Software Advice, Like Sew); a quilt shop said Rain does not always record fabric sales properly (Capterra page 2). Inventory bugs reported "periodically".
- Support: "Tech support is lacking...may take weeks or months to get answers"; "When it does not work we are basically not able to function" (https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/449886-Like-Sew/). Cloud only, so no internet means no sales.
- Accounting: no QuickBooks on the Startup plan; "requires QuickBooks exports".

### Like Sew sew-and-vac side
- Work orders from drop-off to pickup with service notes, parts used, machine history, and automatic text updates (https://likesew.com/sew-and-vac). Serialized inventory and work orders are on the top Plus plan (https://likesew.com/pricing). Reviews of the repair side from sew-and-vac owners: not found (Rain reviews praise repair tracking mostly from music stores).
- Like Sew bought POSIM and discontinued it, per a search summary of review pages; I could not confirm this on a primary page, so treat as unverified.

## 2. Other products

Fabric handling and reorder features per vendor claims. Price as listed publicly.

- **PayGo (quilt-shop POS, 14+ years).** Claims it "tracks bolts of fabric and handles ordering, cutting, fractions, fat quarters, kits and bolt style price labels", plus purchase orders, vendor catalogs, product imaging, classes with rosters, loyalty, and a Shopify link for synced stock. From $1,200, then $120 per month after year one for one workstation, $70 per month per extra workstation; cloud (AWS) or local server. https://paygopos.com/quilt-shop-pos/ . The only one that names bolts and fat quarters outright. No reviews found, so quality unknown.
- **POSIM.** Yards, short bolts and fat quarters; kit builder; recurring billing for Block of the Month; classes; repair work orders; QuickBooks and others; integrates with Moda, Island Batik, Checker and Brewer (the only product naming fabric wholesalers). Custom quote. https://posim.com/business-types/fabric-and-quilt-store-pos-system/ . Status after Like Sew's purchase unverified (see above).
- **Stride Retail (Logan, Utah).** Fractional yard sales, tracks purchases by the yard, reserve inventory levels to stop online overselling, Block of the Month and subscription boxes, quilt-shop website templates, sales and profitability reports. Price not found. https://strideretail.com/industries-served/sewing-and-quilting . Reviews not found.
- **MicroBiz.** Fractional yards, matrix products, tiered pricing, special and back orders, reorder point alerts, CSV import, vendor catalog search by SKU/UPC/name that pulls "images and pricing data" in one click. Price not found. https://www.microbiz.com/sewing-fabric-store-pos-system/
- **AIM (Tri-Technical Systems).** Serialized, print, kits and package deals, color and size matrices, 200+ reports, classes, repairs. Price on request. https://www.sewingpointofsalesoftware.com/
- **RetailEdge.** General craft and hobby POS with kitting; software from $495 one time, support plans extra. No fabric-by-the-yard claim seen. https://www.rainpos.com/blog/craft-store-point-of-sale-software ; https://www.capterra.com/p/76636/RetailEdge/ (price via search summary)
- **Square for Retail.** Free, Plus $49, Premium $149 per month; processing 2.6% + 15 cents in person (https://www.nerdwallet.com/business/software/reviews/square-for-retail, via search summary). Fractions are a workaround: a quilt shop owner asked in March 2025 how to sell half and quarter yards; the answer was "set Inventory by 1/4 and create your sizes and prices by 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and by the yard", with doubt about which Square product allows it (https://community.squareup.com/t5/Online-Store/selling-fabric-by-the-yard/m-p/785822). No bolts, no fabric vendor feeds seen. Purchase orders on paid tiers: not verified on a primary page.
- **Lightspeed Retail.** $89, $149, $289 per month billed annually, $59 per extra register (https://posbull.com/pricing/lightspeed-retail, via search summary). Strong general POs, receiving and serials, but no fractional-yard or bolt concept found.
- **Shopify POS.** POS Pro $89 per location per month on top of a store plan from $29 (https://www.shopify.com/blog/pos-system-cost). Good website, general inventory only. Quilt shops use it as the website side (PayGo links to it, Like Sew Plus integrates with it).
- **Clover.** Retail plans from about $16 per month on a hardware bundle, card rates 2.3% to 2.6% + 10 cents (https://tech.co/pos-system/clover-pos-pricing). No fabric features found.
- **ShopKeep.** Not researched in depth; no quilt-specific page found. Not found.
- **CheckinPOS** lists a quilt-shop page (https://www.checkinpos.com/quilt-shop-pos); it would not load (server error). Not found.
- **Newer quilt startups.** None found that are POS or shop-operations software. Adjacent tools only: PreQuilt (quilt design tool where shops can upload collections, https://prequilt.com/) and QuiltBase (fabric collection reference database indexed by manufacturer, designer, year, SKU, colourway, https://www.quiltbase.com/; "a reference database, not a shop").
- **DIY.** One fabric shop owner on a podcast: "I do keep the inventory in a separate spreadsheet and it is time consuming", and her Etsy and Shopify tools do not "talk to each other" for bundles that share fabrics (https://quilterscandy.com/93/amp/, date not shown).

## 3. Pain points (with sources)

Caution: the clearest pain-point lists are on Like Sew's own blog, which sells a fix. They match what reviewers say, but weigh accordingly.

- **Bolt yardage unknown.** "How much is actually left on that bolt?" and owners "guessing every time, and sometimes you guess wrong mid-cut" (https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shop-inventory, vendor-authored).
- **Counts.** "notes get shuffled, and suddenly you're not sure where you left off. So you start over, again" (same). Reviewer: "Inventory management at year-end needs improvement" (Software Advice). Craft Industry Alliance quotes a 28-year shop owner that "keeping control of inventory" is the top takeaway (https://craftindustryalliance.org/chapter-nine-managing-your-merch/, members-only; seen through search summary only).
- **Counts that drift.** When sales are not scanned, "your system thinks you have more fabric than you do" (Like Sew blog). Quilt shop review: Rain "doesn't always record fabric sales properly" (Capterra).
- **Special orders lost.** A customer's special-order fabric sold without a note, then the owner scrambles for substitutes (Like Sew blog).
- **Slow fabric and cash tied up.** "if a product isn't selling, it has to go to make room for something that will" and "the first markdown you take is always the cheapest markdown" (American Quilt Retailer, https://www.americanquiltretailer.com/taking-the-mystery-out-of-markdowns/). Walls of bolts "tying up cash" (Like Sew blog on curation, https://likesew.com/blog/quilt-shops-fewer-skus). Suggested aging bands: 0 to 90 days reorder winners, 181 to 365 days stop reorders and test markdowns (a Like Sew article via search summary; one source, a rule of thumb).
- **Reordering collections that sell out.** Moda reportedly does not reprint lines (search summary; a 2008 blog comment asked whether lines "ever" reprint, https://blog.fatquartershop.com/notes-from-moda-2/). So the pain is deciding fast what to reorder while the collection is still available. Owner quotes on this: not found.
- **Seasonal timing.** "winter flannels are still out in April, your spring florals arrive late because you didn't reorder in time" (Like Sew blog).
- **Precuts and fat quarters.** Only vendor-side mentions found (counts for fat quarters that sell in store). Owner quotes: not found.
- **Kits and BOM clubs.** Shops run many clubs, but I found no owner complaints about tracking them. Rain gating "Subscriptions (BOM)" to its top plan is the only signal. Owner quotes: not found.
- **Online sync.** Customers "purchasing items that have already sold out" when channels are out of step (Like Sew blog); Stride sells "reserve inventory levels" as the fix. Rain reviewers like the sync but dislike the site builder.
- **Reports they wish they had.** Vendor claim: "reports that don't tell owners what they actually need to know" (Like Sew, via search summary). Reviewers: slow year-long reports, a sales-tax report that does not split non-taxable items. A fabric-specific report (sell-through by collection, bolt age, reorder list) was not requested in any owner quote I found.
- **Data entry of new fabric (SKUs, photos).** Reviewer value is "only having to load a product once". Like Sew launched AI descriptions because writing them by hand was the limit. MicroBiz pulls images and prices from vendor catalogs. Notions Marketing is the one wholesaler feed named for Rain. No source shows a collection-level import (one collection, all bolts, all colourways, photos).
- **Switching costs.** A shop owner ran Rain "for a year", found it expensive, and went back; another was told on cancelling that "things were not completed" (Capterra page 2). Setup said to run "$2,000+" (one report). Because stock, customers and the website sit in one system, a switch is a full rebuild. No public data on how many switch.

## 5. The gap and where a newcomer could win

What looks weak or missing across all of them (based on claims; I could not test any product):

1. **Collection-first receiving.** No page describes entering a collection once (all colourways, bolts, SKUs, photos, prices) from a wholesaler feed. Only Notions Marketing (Rain) and Moda, Island Batik, Checker and Brewer (POSIM) are named. This is the data-entry pain and the most credible opening.
2. **Bolt life tracking.** Only PayGo and POSIM name bolts. Nobody shows a bolt's remaining yardage, a bolt-end flag, or a "cut it down to a remnant or fat quarters" move. Fits the "bolt mystery" pain.
3. **Reorder decisions for collections.** POs exist, but no page shows suggested reorders from collection sell-through, timing against how long a collection stays available, or "stop reordering, mark down" aging. The AQR markdown advice is done by hand.
4. **Precut and fat quarter counts.** Often a unit price. Breaking a bolt into cut counts, or counting a precut bundle as its parts, is not described.
5. **Kits and BOM tied to fabric.** Kits are claimed everywhere, but nothing shows a kit or club month reserving the bolts or yardage it needs, or warning when a sold-out collection will break a later month. Rain gates BOM behind its top plan.
6. **Honest pricing and exit.** Rain hides Core and Plus prices, and reviewers mention cancellation friction and setup costs. A clear price and easy data export would stand out.
7. **Reports.** Complaints are about slow big reports and a poor tax report. Fabric reports (sell-through by collection and designer, age of stock, margin by cut type) are not described by anyone.

Where a newcomer should not fight: general payments, hardware, and website building.

Open questions I could not answer from public sources: real market share; quilt-shop owners' own words on fat quarters, BOM clubs and reprint timing (owner forums and Facebook groups did not surface); and how well PayGo, Stride, MicroBiz and POSIM actually perform (no public reviews found).
